# [DOA Estimation] Domain-Adaptive DOA Estimation in Multipath Interference Channels [with MATLAB Code]

Currently, two main challenges in the DoA estimation of products are interference sources and multipath channels. Interference sources may mask the desired source, while the multipath channel between the desired source and the phased array can lead to deviations in the DoA estimation of the desired source. The core method to overcome these challenges is to understand the environment before localization. For example, this approach was first introduced in [6] for acoustic source localization with a predefined grid of potential source positions. In the context of communication signals, similar methods based on fingerprinting have been proposed in [7]-[9]. Recently, this method has been used to mitigate multipath effects in [10], where the DoA estimation formula is transformed into a classification problem. Positioning methods based on manifold learning were also proposed in [11]-[13]. However, all these methods inherently rely on the environment and require labeled samples, i.e., signal measurements associated with known DoA. Another recent method is based on Riemannian geometry [14]. This algorithm considers interference sources in reverberant environments and proposes a beamformer design and interference suppression method based on Riemannian geometry. However, it is assumed that the interference sources are only partially active. Also, methods for super-resolution have been proposed [15], [16]. However, to suppress interference, they need to identify different sources, which requires not always readily available prior knowledge. In recent years, with the development of deep learning (DL), many DL-based methods have been introduced for robust DoA estimation [17]-[22]. Additionally, various variants have been proposed, such as time-frequency masking integrated into DL methods to alleviate noise and interference [23]-[25]. However, DL methods generally require large datasets for training models, which are not always readily available. Moreover, DL methods usually exhibit higher computational complexity than classical signal processing methods.
The study addresses the DoA estimation problem for an expected source in a known interest area, given interference sources and multipath conditions. By leveraging prior knowledge of the known interest area and the available positions of the phased array, we can mitigate adverse conditions without the need for any labeled samples (pre-recorded signals). Inspired by domain adaptation in machine learning and parallel transmission in Riemannian geometry, we generate a set of simulated steering vectors using the free space model. This collection is used to construct a mapping applied to the received signal, mapping them from the adverse environment to the reference environment without interference and multipath effects. Subsequently, within the reference environment, we employ the selected DoA estimation algorithm on the mapped signals rather than the received ones.
Generally speaking, our method introduces two novel perspectives for DoA estimation. First, it is similar to domain adaptation (DA), a longstanding issue in machine learning that has been extensively studied [26]-[30]. We consider complex steering vectors lying in Euclidean space CM, where M is the number of elements in the phased array, and we specify two domains. The reference domain is defined by the simulated steering vectors and represents a free space environment devoid of multipath and interference sources. The operating domain is determined by the received (noisy) signals. We design a map that alleviates the impact of multipath and interference from the reference domain of the operating domain.
From a geometric perspective, we investigate the sample correlation matrix of the received noisy signal and consider it in conjunction with the model correlation matrix produced by reference steering vectors in the space of M × M Hermitian positive definite (HPD) matrices. We show that our established mapping can be similarly described as parallel transmission in an HPD manifold equipped with affine-invariant metrics [31]-[35]. This mapping highlights the inherent shortcomings of the original DoA estimation algorithm designed for the ideal covariance matrix, such as the overall covariance matrix originating from a single source without interference or multipath propagation in free space. However, these covariance matrices are often unavailable in practice. Therefore, we propose a method to map real-world covariance matrices from the operational domain to the reference domain representing the ideal covariance matrix.
The main contributions of this paper are as follows. Firstly, we introduce a novel approach focused on DOA estimation with interference suppression under adverse conditions. The proposed method learns from an environment without prior signal recording or access to it, making it applicable to various applications. The results of the proposed method are presented in a linear mapping, exhibiting low complexity and being mathematically tractable. Moreover, it is a general method that can be applied to various DoA estimation methods.
Secondly, our experiments demonstrate that the proposed method improves DoA estimation in environments with interference sources and multipath effects using a baseline beamformer. In the experiments, we considered both acoustic signals and RF (radio frequency) signals, proving the applicability of our method in a wide range of applications. Although our method is general and suitable for various downstream DoA estimation methods, our focus is on three standard beamformers: delay and sum (DS) beamformer [36], [37], minimum variance uncorrelated linear estimate (MVULE) beamformer [38] and multiple signal classification (MUSIC) beamformer [39]-[41]. These three beamformers are considered because they are widely used and demonstrate different approaches to estimate DoA: statistical methods (DS), minimization of the target function (MVULE), and subspace methods (MUSIC). Importantly, our method is not limited to these beamformers; we also applied it to the proposed zero-crossing beam pattern design method in [42], and showed that it leads to improved results. Additionally, we studied the SRP of the proposed method combined with the DS beamformer and showed that it inherently includes nulls in the direction of the interference source, meaning that the energy of the sample correlation matrix in this direction decreases.
Thirdly, we conducted theoretical analysis of the proposed method. We show that the second order statistics map matched to the reference environment represents the model typically considered by DoA estimation methods. Moreover, we show that for equal power expected and interference sources, and under certain conditions, the SRP of the DS beamformer is higher than that of the directional expected source compared to the directional interference source.
Conclusions This work suggests that the DoA estimation of a source is required in the presence of interference sources and multipath. We utilize prior knowledge of the known interested region and the position of the phased array to compute the mapping applied to the received signal before the DoA estimation. This mapping is considered as domain adaptation, enabling signals to adapt from the noise domain to the reference environment, thereby mitigating interference sources and multipath. The proposed method can be applied to various DoA estimation methods. We theoretically and empirically show that the proposed method improves acoustic and RF signal DoA estimation using three commonly used beamformers.
